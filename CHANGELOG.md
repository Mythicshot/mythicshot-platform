# Changelog

Every merged fix or update gets the next version number (0.1.1, 0.1.2, and so on) and one entry here, newest first.

## 0.1.61 (2026-11-23)

- Removal after a trial or an ended subscription, with billing on only (a site without the Stripe settings is unchanged in every answer). Links come down: a trial's published tours, shared images and clips answer visitors exactly as unpublished ones from the moment it ends, an ended subscription's 30 days after it ended, decided on every request in the lookup each public route already makes, so no call is added; nothing is changed or deleted, and a subscription makes them answer again at once.
- The removal day is the first full UTC day after the 90 days. Three warning mails go to the account's own address 30, 7 and 1 days before it, in the approved words with the sentence about downloading everything first; each is recorded once the mail server took it (under a digest of the address), never sent twice for the same day, and tried again at the next sweep when mail is off or the server refused it. A late mail names a later day, and the removal never comes before a day a mail named. A changed address, or a new end of the subscription, starts the warnings over. The dashboard shows the same day, in UTC.
- Removal itself is off unless the new setting `BILLING_REMOVAL=on` is present. With it, a workspace is removed only when every rule in the README's list holds at that moment: on the clock (never exempt, the admin's, an existing account, one with an allowance set by hand, or the site's own), its day come, all three warnings accepted (the first at least 29 days and the last at least 20 hours before), its address unchanged, Stripe saying definitely that the subscription ended and every checkout expired, no export in progress, and at most 5 removals a run and 20 a day. It disables the account and records the removal in one guarded step, then removes everything with an admin's delete's own steps; stopped half-way it leaves nothing reachable and is finished by the next sweep. Every step is in the audit record, ids and counts only.
- The sweep runs from the admin command `billing-sweep` (with `--dry-run`, which lists what it would remove and changes nothing), to be run daily; the README shows a daily entry for the server's crontab, and no timer runs inside the app. On the Worker target the same sweep is a scheduled handler within one invocation's budget, written and tested but not wired to any trigger.
- An admin can undo a delete that has removed nothing yet (`POST /api/v1/admin/accounts/<id>/undo-removal`, recorded as `account_removal_undone`), as when Stripe never answered the delete's cancel: the account stays disabled and can be enabled again. The Admin page has no button for it yet.
- Migration 0024 adds `billing_warning`, the record of the warning mails, and `account_removal.work_started_at`, the mark a removal gets before it removes anything. It is additive, and 0023 is left free.
- After the security review, before removal is ever switched on: any subscription on record that is not definitely over (`unpaid`, `paused`, `incomplete`, as well as live ones for an admin's delete) is cancelled at Stripe before anything of the workspace goes, by the sweep and by an admin's delete alike, and nothing goes until Stripe answers `canceled`. Before every pass of a sweep's removal the workspace is read again and Stripe asked again: a subscription that went live, a checkout made after the start or still open, or a workspace that left the clock stops the removal for good before anything is cancelled or deleted (`billing_removal_stopped`, a billing problem for the admin); Subscribe is refused while a removal is recorded, also when the removal began while Stripe was making the checkout. An undo that lands while a removal or a delete asks Stripe is honoured. Migration 0024 also adds `account_removal.stopped_at`.
- A subscription whose first payment never completed (`incomplete_expired`) is no longer an ended subscription: the workspace stays on its trial, with the trial's allowance, writes, links, mails and removal day. This changes the read-only rule of 0.1.57 for that case, on purpose.
- The sweep: one at a time (a second exits at once), at most 50 warning mails a run (those due longest first), the ids of workspaces with an unconfirmed warning in its summary and `billing-sweep --clear-claim <workspace id>` to clear one (audited), nothing removed when the server's clock and Stripe's differ by more than an hour, old checkout records cleared, a dry run that writes nothing at all, and a line in the dry run when pictures come from a public bucket (whose pictures stay reachable when links come down; documented). A workspace with more than one account is on no clock. An undo of a sweep's removal clears its warnings. An ended workspace with an allowance set by hand shows no removal dates.
- The ended subscription's 7-day mail now reads "On <date> your tours, images and clips are removed." (approved by the owner on 2026-11-24: its links came down earlier); the trial's is unchanged.
- The Stripe client also returns each answer's Date header, and sends a GET's fields as its query.
- After the second security review: a checkout record is never cleared on its age. The sweep asks Stripe about each (at most 10 a run): one confirmed expired is cleared; one Stripe says was paid but never linked holds the removal for good, is linked as its notice would have and is a billing problem (`billing_checkout_paid_unlinked`); the publish housekeeping no longer clears checkout records at all (the 0.1.58 behaviour is gone, on purpose). An `incomplete` subscription holds the sweep (never cancelled). A removal the sweep stopped can be undone even after files went, answering how many were removed. After a cancel for a removal or a delete, the subscription's open invoices are voided (to be checked against a Stripe sandbox). The sweep renews its lock as it goes.
- With 0.1.60 merged: the notices say where everything can be downloaded, with "Account page" a link to the "Download all my data" card, and the mails' download link points there too (`/account#h-data`). A download in progress holds a removal, asked before the start and before every pass; the download requests refuse with `409 deleting` while a removal is recorded; a download's leftover record goes with the workspace. The trial notice names the removal day ("On <date> your content is permanently removed unless you subscribe. You can download everything from your Account page.", approved by the owner) only while `BILLING_REMOVAL` is on, which the app now reads for that wording alone.
- The README has new sections, "Removal after 90 days" and "The billing sweep", and the setting; the container runbook lists the new audit events. New tests: `test/billing-removal.test.ts` on both targets (the clock against its SQL, the six mails' exact words and dates, late and failed mails, removal on its day, a workspace that subscribes on its last day, a clock that goes back or jumps ahead, an interrupted removal, Stripe's answers at the last check, checkouts, caps, the dry run, the setting unset, billing off, every exempt kind, links down byte for byte, the dashboard's dates, undoing a delete, and the Worker's sweep), `test/node-billing-sweep-command.test.ts`, `test/node-migration-0024.test.ts`, and additions to the isolation, host, API description, contract and budget suites.

## 0.1.60 (2026-11-23)

- Download all my data: the Account page has a new card, in every billing state and whether billing is on or off, read-only workspaces included, and whether or not the account has agreed to the site's current terms (a disabled account cannot sign in, so it cannot download), with the approved wording and a Download button. It gives one zip file, `mythicshot-data.zip`, with every tour (a folder with its pictures, its logo, its chapters, steps and settings as `tour.json`, and `index.html`, the tour's draft as a click-through page that opens from disk with no server and no network), every shared image and clip as its own file, and `README.txt` and `account.json`, which list each tour, image and clip with its title, dates, whether it is published, who may view it and its size, and the account's email address, username and workspace. Nothing of another account or workspace, and no token, session, key or audit record.
- Three requests, a browser session's alone (never a token or a connected computer): `POST /api/v1/account/data`, with the dashboard's anti-forgery header, answers a one-use address good for a minute; `GET /api/v1/account/data/<ticket>` claims the address first, then sends the zip as a stream, written file by file from the private store as the browser takes it, each tour's page built only when it is reached, stored without compression, with ZIP64 where a size or count needs it; an address that cannot be followed answers the dashboard's failure sentence as plain text, never a file, and a HEAD uses none. `GET /api/v1/account/data` says whether a download is still going, so the button stays disabled until it ends. One download per workspace at a time, 5 asks an hour per workspace (`429 rate_limited`), one audit row, `data_exported`, for each download started, and a download stopped after 5 minutes with nothing taken or 6 hours in all. The three are left out of the API description as the Account page's own. The links in the lists are full addresses from the settings.
- The zip is never held whole: every row comes from one statement, and every file is read by the key its own row names. On Cloudflare every stored file is one of the request's 40 calls, so a download there holds at most 35 stored files (a step picture, a logo, or a 2,000,000-byte part of an image or clip), and a larger workspace is refused with `409 too_large_to_export` before any byte is sent. The Node server has no such limit.
- The tour player also takes "." as its picture base, for a page opened from disk, with pictures named `pictures/<name>.png` or `.jpg` beside it; nothing else changes for published pages.
- The README has a new section, "Download all my data", and the Docker runbook lists the new audit event. New tests: the zip writer read back by a reader written apart from it (`test/zip.test.ts`, `test/zip-reader.ts`), the download's contents, names, pages, refusals, one-at-a-time rule, rate limit, read-only workspace and limit on both targets (`test/export.test.ts`), the Account page's card, and the routes in the isolation, terms-gate, host, budget and API-description suites; on the Node server, a download larger than a Worker could send, and the zip unpacked by the unzip programs the system ships (`test/node-export.test.ts`).

## 0.1.59 (2026-11-23)

- Billing: Subscribe works with Stripe's real answers. An embedded checkout's client secret is about 468 characters and holds percent-encoded bytes, which 0.1.58 refused (and so answered `502 stripe_unavailable`), found in the owner's first sandbox run. The check now takes any printable ASCII up to 2,048 characters but control characters, spaces, quotes, angle brackets and backslashes, at create and when an open checkout is offered again; the secret is still only ever handed, JSON-encoded, to the workspace's own session.
- An answer of Stripe refused for its shape now writes one audit row, `billing_answer_refused`, naming the call and the field and never the value, and counts among the admin summary's `billing_problems`.
- The site asks Stripe once for the configured price with the configured key (at the Node server's start, on a Worker's first request): a price Stripe says does not exist stops the start, naming STRIPE_PRICE_ID and STRIPE_API_KEY; a key that may not read prices does not, and the admin summary's new `billing_price` says it could not be checked. The README recommends Prices: read for the key.
- The local exception is for exactly localhost and 127.0.0.1: the IPv6 loopback address could never be configured as the site's address, and is gone from the docs and the rule.
- The tests' fake Stripe now answers in the shapes the sandbox did (66-character checkout ids, 468-character secrets, real-looking subscription, customer, invoice and event ids, embedded and Managed Payments fields), and new tests pin the sandbox's order of notices at a payment and a portal cancellation that sets cancel_at with the flag false.

## 0.1.58 (2026-11-23)

- Billing, before it is switched on anywhere (it stays off without the Stripe settings, exactly as in 0.1.57): a notice of a subscription not linked to a workspace is held back only while it may really be one a checkout will link (made in the last 2 hours, and for an unknown customer only with a checkout of the site made before it that had not expired more than 10 minutes earlier); every other one, such as the notice of a subscription cancelled by an account's delete, is taken at once.
- A checkout now lives from 32 to 33 minutes (the start of its minute plus 33), never at or under Stripe's 30-minute minimum, with one idempotency key per minute.
- Deleting an account records the delete first, so the account cannot be enabled meanwhile, then cancels the workspace's subscription and closes every checkout of it that has not expired; one that turns out paid has its subscription cancelled too. Without Stripe's definite answer nothing is deleted. Disabling an account still cancels nothing, by the owner's decision: a disable can be undone.
- The settings: some but not all of the four Stripe settings now stop the start, naming the missing ones (none is still off), and the two keys must be of one mode, both test or both live. For a run on one's own computer against a Stripe sandbox, an address of exactly localhost or 127.0.0.1 over http is taken for Stripe's return addresses when both keys are test keys; nothing else is relaxed.
- A stale subscription is read back from Stripe at most once a minute per workspace, keeping the last outcome in between; one Stripe confirms live with an old period end is trusted for a day, with one audit row, `billing_stale_confirmed`. Reads of the billing state are counted too, 600 an hour per workspace. Migration 0022 adds the three columns this needs; it is additive.
- The record of checkouts is cleared 7 days after each expired, by the workspace's own housekeeping. A paid checkout that named a workspace's checkout but not its reference or customer is now among the admin summary's `billing_problems`.
- The README and the Docker runbook describe the changes. New tests: `test/billing-round3.test.ts`, `test/node-migration-0022.test.ts`, and the earlier billing tests brought to the new rules.

A stored checkout is replaced only once Stripe confirms it expired, every checkout the site made is recorded so a paid one always finds its workspace, and the admin's summary counts the billing problems to look at. Deleting an account cancels its subscription at Stripe first. Billing needs the site's own https address and counts its requests to Stripe per workspace.

Manage subscription opens Stripe's customer portal. The listener, `POST /api/v1/billing/webhook`, checks Stripe's signature by hand, takes each notice once, matches a workspace only by ids the site stored, and reads the subscription back from Stripe before storing anything.
- The dashboard shows the approved notices, the Billing section of the Account page and the page Stripe sends a person back to. The Subscribe page is the only page whose Content-Security-Policy allows Stripe; every other page's is unchanged.

The README has a new section, "Billing (optional)", the Docker runbook lists the new audit events, and the settings pass through the tunnel stack and the cPanel startup file. New tests cover the settings switch, every state's allowance and refusal, the checkout's parameters, the signature, repeated, out-of-order and mismatched notices, the policy of every page, and billing in the isolation, terms-gate, host and budget suites.

## 0.1.53 (2026-11-21)

- A site can now ask everyone who signs in to agree to its terms before they use the dashboard, and keeps a record of who agreed to which version and when. A site that has published no terms asks nothing and answers every request as before.
- The operator publishes terms from a plain-text file with the server command: `terms publish <file>` (`-` reads standard input), `terms list` and `terms show <id>`, also as `cpanel.sh admin terms ...`, which now tells the command the folder it was run in so a relative file name is found there. The format is strict and has no markup (`docs/terms-example.txt`): `title:`, `version:`, up to 3 `link:` lines (strict https addresses), `main points:` with 1 to 12 bullets of at most 300 characters, and `terms:` with the full terms (headings, bullets and paragraphs, at most 200,000 bytes). Control, separator and direction characters are refused, and a file that breaks a rule is refused with one line that names the line. Publishing the same content twice is refused (its SHA-256 is unique), and of two publishes at the same moment the second gets one line and changes nothing. A stored version is served as it was published: read back, a link today's rule refuses is left out and every text is as stored, so a later, stricter rule never makes the terms unreadable. "I agree" takes its JSON body by the rule every session change's body has (labelled `application/json`). On Cloudflare the screens and the storage work, but there is no server command yet, so terms cannot be published there.
- Migration 0018 adds `terms_version` (the title, the version's label, the main points, the links, the full terms, the SHA-256 and the publish time, each version kept as published: two triggers refuse any change or delete) and `terms_agreement` (the account, the version and the time, nothing else). It is additive; an older build names neither, and going back needs nothing.
- Until a signed-in account agrees to the current version, every request made with its session answers `403 terms_required` and does nothing, the admin's too, but `GET /api/v1/terms`, `GET /api/v1/terms/agreed`, `POST /api/v1/terms/agree` and the two sign-outs. The check is read in the session's own lookup, so it costs no database call; tokens are never held and answer byte for byte as before. Every dashboard page then goes to the new terms screen, `/terms`, with "Before you start", the main points, the full terms in a box that scrolls on its own, the links, "Sign out" and "I agree"; it comes back with new terms at each account's next request. "I agree" records the version that was shown, only while it is still the current one (else `409 terms_changed` and the new version is shown), once, with the audit row `terms_agreed` (ids only).
- The Account page says "You agreed to the terms of <version> on <date>." with "Read them", which opens that version read-only at `/terms?read`; `GET /api/v1/session` gives `terms_agreed` only to an account that has agreed to one. The Admin page has a "Terms" column, shown on a site that has published terms: the version and date of an agreement to the current terms, else "Not yet", with "Agreed to <older version> on <date>" under it for an agreement to an older one. The admin API's list of accounts gives each account's `terms` (the version's label, the time and whether it is `current`, or null) and the site's `current_terms` (its label, or null). Deleting an account removes its agreements; its audit rows stay.
- The API description documents the three requests and `terms_required` once, among the answers any request can get. The README, the cPanel guide, the Docker and Cloudflare runbooks and the dashboard's browser check describe the terms.
- New tests: every route of the app is classified for the terms and every session route is proved held before agreeing and free after (`test/terms-gate.test.ts`, which fails for a new route nobody classified); the rules, the stored row and audit row, the version shown, new terms, the body and anti-forgery checks, tokens, viewers, the admin, untrusted text and account deletion (`test/terms.test.ts`); the file format (`test/terms-file.test.ts`); the pages' markup and sentences and their scripts against a stand-in document and the real app; the server command; migration 0018 on a site from before it, with terms published after the upgrade; and the isolation suite's own case for agreements.
